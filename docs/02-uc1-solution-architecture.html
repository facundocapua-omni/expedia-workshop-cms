01Six design positions, taken up front

Each of these answers one of the open questions. If EG disagrees with any of them the design changes shape, so they belong at the top of the conversation rather than buried in a component description.

Q1Contentful stores the message template, never the partner's number

A proof-point entry holds localised copy with named variables — not "seen 47 times" but "Your property page was seen {views} times in the last {windowDays} days". The value arrives with NBA's decision and is substituted at resolution time.

This is what keeps the content set at variant scale — dozens of entries — instead of partner scale. No entry anywhere holds a partner identifier or a partner's metric.

Grammatical agreement between the value and the sentence around it is out of scope for the POC. See the deferred-scope note below, because it is not a detail.

Q2NBA selects the variant; Contentful never decides

NBA reads a variant index published out of Contentful — every variant with its segment tags, tone, available channels and translated locales — and picks one. The content platform is asked for a specific thing by identifier and returns it. No audience rules, no conditional blocks, no personalisation engine inside the CMS.

This matches the pilot parameters as written, keeps the personalisation licence out of the critical path, and makes the fetch deterministic and cacheable. The cost is a new obligation the source document doesn't mention: NBA cannot choose between variants it has never been told about, so the index feed is a first-class deliverable, not plumbing.

Q3One content reference everywhere; channel is a parameter, not a different ID

All channels quote the same reference. The channel is passed alongside it, and selects which channel rendering is returned. This makes both statements in the source document true at once: every surface is fetching the same content, and each surface gets copy shaped for it.

It also gives the consistency test something to assert against — every response carries a consistencyKey derived from variant, version, locale and parameters, and identical keys across channels is a machine-checkable pass.

newA thin resolution service sits between Contentful and the channels

Channels do not call the Contentful delivery API directly. A small stateless service — the Content Resolution API — parses the reference, serves an immutable published snapshot, applies and reports translation fallback, composes the message, and shapes the channel payload.

This is the one net-new component in the design, and it needs saying plainly: the delivery API alone cannot satisfy three of the stated requirements. It serves only the latest published version, so "exactly the same content" breaks the moment an author republishes mid-campaign. Its locale fallback is silent, so a consumer cannot report on it. And it will not survive a burst of concurrent fetches after a newsletter send without a cache in front of it. If the guarantee is that every channel gets identical content, that guarantee has to be enforced in one place — otherwise the pilot proves consistency of input, not of output.

Q6bTone belongs to the partner; register belongs to the surface

Tone — educational, familiar, data-led — is a selection criterion. NBA chooses it per partner from engagement history, and it stays constant as the partner moves from the email to the portal to a conversation with their Market Manager. That persistence is the benefit EG's own step 5 describes.

Register is the opposite: length, formality, and who is being addressed. It is fixed per surface and identical for every partner, so it lives on the channel rendering and is never selected. Alongside it sits audience — partner or internal — because the CRM rendering does not address the partner at all. It briefs a Market Manager on what the partner has already seen, which is a different piece of writing with different review criteria.

Channel therefore never enters variant selection. It is supplied on the fetch, picks a rendering, and acts as an availability filter on the index — a constraint, not a criterion. Section 03 sets the four dimensions out side by side.

deferredGrammatical agreement is out of POC scope, and has to be solved before production

For the POC, copy is plain text with named variables and a straight substitution at resolution time. Authors write one sentence per locale with no syntax beyond the variable itself. Number formatting follows the locale — thousands separators and decimal marks — but nothing in the sentence adapts to the value.

What this knowingly leaves broken. A value of 1 produces "seen 1 times". German and Thai degrade the same way. Arabic is worse: six plural categories, with the noun and any surrounding adjective changing alongside the number, so one sentence cannot be correct across the range. Ordinals and any gendered noun driven by the variable have the same problem.

Why it is acceptable here and nowhere else. The POC tests whether one authored source reaches every channel consistently, translated and versioned. Substitution proves that. But CMS-47 — composing the metric into a grammatically correct, locale-appropriate sentence — is one of the capabilities EG is evaluating, and the one they describe as a capability gain rather than an efficiency gain. Deferring it means that capability is demonstrated in principle, not proven, and the readout should say so rather than let it pass unremarked.

What full implementation needs. A plural-aware message format — ICU is the standard and every translation tool already parses it — with authoring that keeps the syntax away from the author: a plain source sentence expanded into plural categories by a generation step, approved once, and stored in the machine-readable form. Two things drive the cost: whether the real parameter types are counts only or also currency, dates and ordinals, and whether Arabic stays in the locale set. Four of the five pilot locales need at most two forms; Arabic alone accounts for most of the work.

02Content model

Four content types plus taxonomy. Variants are modelled as referenced entries rather than a variant dimension, because core entries have no such dimension — this is the hand-modelled route described in the backlog, and it is deliberate here rather than a fallback.

Figure 1 — Content model. The variant is the addressable unit: it is what NBA chooses and what the content reference points at.

Why the variant is the addressable unit

NBA's decision is "this segment, this tone" — so the thing it selects has to be the thing identified. Making the base proof point addressable would force a second resolution step somewhere, and the only candidates are Contentful (ruled out by Q2) or each channel independently (which is the drift problem the pilot exists to solve). Channel and locale are narrower: they don't change which message is being told, only how it is dressed, so they belong as parameters on the fetch rather than in the identity.

Approval state on the entry

The approvals[] field records who signed off on what and when — variant copy, per-locale translation, channel rendering. It exists because the audit requirement is about editorial accountability, not just field history: "who approved the Japanese version of the familiar-tone variant" is the question that gets asked, and version history alone answers it awkwardly.

03The content reference

The identifier the whole use case rests on. It does not exist natively — the platform issues an ID per entry, with locale as a query parameter and version as a revision counter — so it has to be defined as a contract and implemented.

// filter when reading the index, never as a selection criterion.
  • The reference is opaque to channels and meaningful to us. SFMC passes it through without parsing; the resolution service decomposes it. Keeping it decomposable matters more than keeping it short — an opaque hash is untraceable at 2am.
  • Version is part of the identity, not metadata. Without it, a republished entry silently changes what a send delivers, and the traceability requirement ("given this ID, show me the exact content") becomes unanswerable for anything historical.
  • Locale and channel travel beside it, not inside it. That is what makes "the same content ID everywhere" literally true across channels, which is the claim the pilot is testing.
  • Parameters are the partner's data and never persist in the CMS. They exist in the decision payload and in the resolved response.

Segment, tone and channel are not the same kind of thing

The source document uses "channel-specific variant" in one place and "variant dimension" in another, which makes channel look like a peer of segment and tone. It isn't. The three sit in different halves of the system, and conflating them is the most expensive modelling mistake available here.

DimensionRoleDecided byWhat it changes
SegmentSelection criterionNBA, from partner attributesWhich message wins. Remove it and every partner receives identical copy — the personalisation thesis collapses.
ToneSelection criterionNBA, from engagement historyHow the same point is framed for this partner — educational, familiar, data-led. A peer of segment; both are inputs to one decision, and it has to survive the move from one channel to the next.
RegisterProperty of the renderingInherited from the channelLength, formality, and who is being addressed. Never selected and never varies by partner.

LocaleRendering parameterThe partner recordWhich translation of the winning message is served. Never selects between variants.

Tone and register are the pair most easily conflated, and the source document uses the word "tone" for both. They pull in opposite directions: tone is chosen per partner and must stay constant across surfaces, while register is fixed per surface and identical for every partner. Keeping them as one field would mean a partner's voice changing every time they move between the email, the portal and a conversation with their Market Manager.

Register carries a second thing the source document implies without stating: the CRM rendering does not address the partner at all. It briefs a Market Manager on what the partner has already seen, so it has to carry the partner's copy and be usable as a talking point. That is why audience sits on the rendering as partner or internal — email and VMC are partner-facing, CRM is not, and generation prompts and review criteria differ accordingly.

Channel constrains without being a criterion: if a variant has no email rendering, NBA shouldn't dispatch it to email — but that absence never makes a different variant a better fit for the partner. It is a filter on the index, not an input to the choice.

Channel still earns its place in the pilot, for a different reason. Without it, cross-channel consistency is trivially true — one string fetched three times proves nothing. With it, the claim becomes that one authored source yields three differently-shaped outputs carrying the same message, which is CMS-01 and CMS-06 tested together. That is why consistencyKey covers variant, version, locale and parameters but deliberately excludes the channel shaping.

04Authoring flow

Four phases, three human gates. Generation is automated; approval never is. Nothing reaches a channel without a named person having accepted it.

Figure 2 — Authoring flow. The design decision worth arguing about is in phase C.

Phase B — generated tone and channel variants

An AI action runs against the approved base template and produces a draft variant per tone, then a draft channel rendering per channel with that channel's constraints applied. The author sees a diff and accepts, edits or rejects. Two things make this safe enough to put in front of a client:

  • Placeholders are validated, not trusted. Generated copy is rejected automatically if it drops, renames or duplicates a parameter from parameterSchema. A tone rewrite that loses {views} is a broken send, and it is the most likely generation failure.
  • Provenance is recorded. generatedBy and the approving user are stored on the entry, so any later review can separate machine-written copy from hand-written copy.

Availability of the AI actions feature on EG's plan needs confirming before this is committed. If it isn't available, the same flow runs through an external generation step writing drafts via the management API — more moving parts, same gates.

Phase C — the translation approval gate

This is where the platform constraint from the backlog bites: publishing acts on the entry, so every locale goes live together, and a per-locale approval gate appears impossible.

The way through is to treat the empty field as the gate. A translation lives in the translation workflow until a reviewer approves it, and only then is it written into the localised field. Unapproved locales stay empty, native fallback serves the source copy, and the resolver flags that a fallback happened. Publishing becomes safe at any moment because the entry never contains unapproved copy.

The trade is that an unapproved locale is not "blocked" — it is served in English and reported as such. For a pilot testing whether partners get consistent content that is the better failure mode, and it turns a modelling problem into a reporting one. It should be stated explicitly to EG rather than discovered at the readout.

06Surfaces and channels

Partner web and VMC are different surfaces, and web is treated as one more channel rather than a special case. Every surface fetches through the same resolution API — that is what keeps the consistency claim true as surfaces are added.

Push and pull are the real difference, not the surface

Email and CRM have a decision pushed to them: NBA decides, and the content reference travels with the send or the record. The pull surfaces have no such moment. A partner opens VMC or a web page at an arbitrary time, and something has to answer what is the current decision for this partner, right now.

Two ways to answer it. NBA exposes a decision lookup the surface calls on page load, or decisions are precomputed per partner and stored for the surfaces to read on NBA's own cadence. The second is materially cheaper and does not put a decisioning engine in a page-render path; the first is fresher. This is a question for EG and it is the one architectural difference between the push and pull surfaces — everything downstream of the decision is identical.

One resolver, every surface — including UC2

The Next.js partner-web application built for Use Case 2 consumes this same Content Resolution API. That has a consequence worth stating plainly rather than discovering during the UC2 build: UC2's content model needs a proof-point block it does not have today, and the two workstreams stop being independent at that point. The block itself is small — a reference to a content reference plus a placement — but the dependency is real and it should appear on both plans.

The upside is the reason for doing it: a fourth surface costs one channel rendering per variant and a client that speaks the same API. It costs nothing in the content model, nothing in the decisioning, and nothing in the consistency guarantee, because all three already treat channel as a parameter rather than a dimension.

08Performance at production volume

The numbers below are engineering estimates of what the proposed design can absorb. EG's real send volumes, click-through rates and latency targets are open questions; until they arrive, these establish the order of magnitude rather than a commitment.

Why the burst is a caching problem and not a CMS problem

The number of distinct resolved payloads is variants × locales × channels × live versions — hundreds in the pilot, low hundreds of thousands at full production scale. It is not a function of the partner base. The only per-partner element is the parameter values, which arrive with the request and are substituted on the way out.

So the content is near-perfectly cacheable, the working set fits in memory on a single node, and a send of any size generates reads against a cache rather than against Contentful. The delivery API is read on publish, never on request.

The headline: even the aggressive burst case sits an order of magnitude below what a single instance handles, and the service is stateless so it scales horizontally anyway. The constraint that actually binds is the delivery API's published rate limits — which is precisely why it is kept off the request path.

Where the cache sits is a hosting decision, not a design one

The snapshot store is already an immutable key-value store addressed by variant, version, locale and channel. That makes it the primary read path and the cache at the same time — there is no separate cache to invalidate, because a published version never changes.

OptionWhere it fitsTrade
In-process memoryInside every resolver instanceSub-millisecond on the hot set. Sized by the working set above, so it holds everything

nginx reverse proxyIn front, inside EG's networkRight answer when the resolver is internal-only and SFMC calls it from inside the perimeter
CDNIn front of internet-facing pull surfacesOnly earns its place for surfaces reachable from the public internet

Recommendation: Redis as the snapshot store and primary read path, with an in-process cache for the hot set. Whether nginx or a CDN sits in front depends on where EG hosts the service and which surfaces are public — a topology decision that belongs with the hosting owner.

09Failure handling and fallback

Two rules underpin the rest. Fallback copy is content, authored and approved like any other content — never a string in the resolver. And the behaviour when content cannot be resolved differs by surface, because the cost of being wrong differs by surface.

Policy per surface

SurfacePolicyBehaviour and reasoning
SFMC emailFail openSend proceeds with the approved fallback variant. A send cannot be recalled, so halting it to protect one module costs more than the module is worth — but the copy that goes out must still be copy someone approved
VMC / partner webFail openPage renders with the fallback variant. A visible page with generic copy beats a broken region
Salesforce CRMFail closedThe module shows nothing. A Market Manager acting on wrong or generic information in front of a partner is worse than having no prompt at all — this is the one surface where absence is safer than approximation

The split is deliberate and worth confirming with EG rather than assuming: it says an internal user is trusted to notice an empty panel, and a partner is not expected to notice generic copy.

Fallback content is authored, not hardcoded

A fallback variant exists per opportunity type, carrying no parameters and making no partner-specific claim. It goes through the same authoring, translation and approval path as everything else, which means it is reviewable, localised into all five locales, and versioned. Copy embedded in the resolver would be none of those things, and it would be the one piece of partner-facing English nobody ever reviews.

The service holds no per-request state and has no side effects, so every one of these is safe to retry. That is a deliberate property, not a convenience: it is what allows a channel to retry a failed fetch without any risk of a partner receiving two different messages for the same decision.

11Validation plan

The seven points you listed, each with the evidence it produces and the condition that decides pass or fail. Every one is machine-checkable except the two where the judgement is genuinely editorial.

Validation pointHow it's exercisedPass condition
Variants of the same content Two proof points, three tones, two segments — twelve variants. Each fetched by its own reference and diffed against the others. Twelve variants resolve independently and correctly from two base proof points, with no duplicated base copy. Adding a thirteenth needs no content-type change.
Automatic generation for tone and channel AI action generates tone variants from the approved base, then channel renderings from each variant — applying that channel's register, audience and constraints — into draft state. Generated copy preserves every parameter in parameterSchema; the placeholder check rejects any output that drops or renames one; the partner tone survives into every channel rendering while the register changes with the surface; the author can accept, edit or reject without leaving the editor; provenance is recorded on the entry.
Translation into five locales Approved variants sent for translation into the four target locales; three returned and approved; one deliberately held back untranslated. Translated copy keeps every variable intact and correctly placed for the target language; the untranslated locale serves source copy with fallbackApplied: true; no other locale is affected; Arabic round-trips without corruption.
Approval workflows Three gates exercised end to end — generated copy, per-locale translation, release — including one rejection at each gate. Nothing unapproved is ever retrievable through the resolution API; a rejection returns the item to draft without affecting published content; each approval records who and when.
Version registry A published variant is fetched, then edited and republished, then fetched again by the original reference. The original reference returns the original copy, byte for byte. The new reference returns the new copy. Both are retrievable, and the diff between versions is inspectable with author and timestamp.
SFMC integration SFMC fetches by reference at send time against the future-state instance and renders into a preview or seed send. Content populates the message from a single call; timeout, unknown reference and retired version each produce a defined, non-breaking behaviour; measured latency and throughput ceiling are documented rather than assumed.
API returns the published version by reference, variant and locale The same reference requested across three channels and five locales, repeatedly, including after a republish. consistencyKey is identical across channels for a given reference, version, locale and parameter set; repeated fetches are byte-identical; the response always states which locale was actually served.

Two judgements stay human and should be scheduled as such: whether a generated tone variant is good, and whether the translated copy reads naturally in each locale. Both need a named reviewer per locale, and neither is a test we can run on EG's behalf. Reviewers should be briefed that grammatical agreement with the variable is out of scope for this round, so they flag translation quality rather than re-reporting a known limitation.

12Alternatives considered

Tone and channel as a single dimension

The argument for it is real: the channel does determine the voice of a communication, so a separate tone dimension could look redundant. Collapsing the two would simplify the model and cut the variant count by roughly a third.

Not adopted, because it works against the source document's thesis rather than simplifying it. Step 5 states that the CRM shows the Market Manager the same tone of voice the partner already received, and the partner walkthrough presents persistent, persona-matched framing as the benefit being tested. If tone is a property of the channel, a partner's voice changes every time they move between the email, the portal and a call — the drift the pilot exists to eliminate.

It would also cost three capability results at once: CMS-19 loses half its pass condition because tone stops being decisioning metadata, CMS-31's criterion collapses from segment plus tone to segment alone, and CMS-06 becomes trivially satisfiable since no cross-channel constant remains.

The observation underneath it is kept: channel does carry a voice, and that voice is modelled as register on the rendering, alongside audience. Should EG's operating model genuinely treat tone as channel-determined, the change is one field plus a re-scoping of those three capabilities — an explicit client decision, which is why it is raised as a question rather than assumed either way.

Channels calling the delivery API directly, no resolution layer

Fewest moving parts and closest to the source document's description. Rejected on three counts: the delivery API serves only the current published version, so version pinning is impossible and a mid-campaign republish silently changes what was sent; locale fallback is silent, so no consumer can report on it; and every channel would have to implement message composition independently, which reintroduces exactly the drift the pilot exists to eliminate.

If EG wants the minimal version anyway, it is viable for the CRM and VMC payload comparison — but not for SFMC, and not for anything claiming immutability.

One entry per partner, holding the partner's metric

The literal reading of the scale note. Rejected: it puts partner data in a content store, makes the entry count a function of the partner base, and makes every metric refresh a content operation. Nothing about it is a content-management problem.

Contentful resolving segment and tone to a variant

Fewer round trips and closer to how personalisation products work. Rejected because it contradicts the stated parameter that NBA decides, pulls a licensed personalisation capability onto the critical path, and makes the fetch non-deterministic and harder to cache. Worth revisiting after the pilot if EG wants decisioning closer to the content.

An opaque hashed content ID

Tidier on the wire. Rejected: an opaque token cannot be read in a log, a support ticket or an SFMC preview, and the traceability requirement is precisely about being able to answer "what was this" long after the send.
