<p class="sec-note">With coexistence by business case, cutover is not a single day but a procedure that repeats for every business case. It has three parts: the tooling that moves the content, the steps that move the traffic, and the checks that protect search.</p>

<h3>Migration tooling</h3>
<div class="fig">

</div>
<p class="cap">Figure 7 — Migration pipeline. Four stages, from the rows the disposition log marks migrate to validated entries in Contentful, into the enhanced content types. Loads are idempotent, so the same pipeline runs from the first rehearsal to the final delta.</p>
<p>A scripted pipeline, built by Globant for this programme and discarded after it (D-31). Two of its properties matter more than the rest:</p>
<ul class="tight">
  <li><strong>Idempotent loads.</strong> Every entry is upserted by a stable ID derived from its AEM path, so the pipeline can run any number of times: full rehearsals first, then delta runs of what changed since the last run, and a final delta immediately before the content freeze.</li>
  <li><strong>Validation on every run.</strong> Counts, required fields, broken links, and a visual diff between the AEM page and the new one.</li>
</ul>

<div class="decide risk">
  <h4><span class="stamp">risk</span>Language copies may not fit one entry</h4>
  <p>AEM keeps each locale as a separate page; Contentful localises field by field inside a single entry. Where the locale versions of a page have drifted apart — a block that exists only in German, a different order in Thai — they do not fit one entry without a decision about which structure wins. Discovery measures how often this happens, and whether URL slugs are localised per locale belongs to the same conversation.</p>
</div>

<h3>SEO protection</h3>
<div class="scroll">
<table>
  <thead><tr><th style="width:20%">Protection</th><th>How</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td><strong>URLs kept by default</strong></td><td>A migrated page keeps its URL and needs no redirect. Only retired and consolidated pages change, plus localised slugs if EG chooses them</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Redirect map</strong></td><td>Generated from the disposition log: 301 to the closest equivalent, 410 when there is none, and no chains. Served by the CDN, so it reaches every visitor during a ramp whichever origin serves them, and switched on at 100% (D-32)</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>On-page parity</strong></td><td>Both versions are crawled and compared URL by URL: title, description, canonical, hreflang for all five locales, structured data, H1, robots directives and internal links</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Baseline</strong></td><td>Organic traffic, top landing pages and backlinks per URL, captured before discovery. A page with backlinks is never retired without its 301</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>During a ramp</strong></td><td>Crawlers are treated like any other visitor, because routing them differently by user agent reads as cloaking. Redirects wait for 100%, so a URL never answers 200 to some visitors and 301 to others</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>After 100%</strong></td><td>Four to six weeks of monitoring: Search Console coverage, 404s in the CDN logs, and organic traffic of key pages against the baseline</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
