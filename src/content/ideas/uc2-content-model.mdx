  <p>The model takes shape in three moves. Five inventory entries share a content model with a parent and become properties on it — media position, background treatment, an optional CTA. Three collection containers share one model and become a single card collection with a layout property. And four entries belong to the structural layer rather than the page: a heading, a section wrapper, a separator and a button, which live on the section container or travel with their parent block.</p>
  <p>Ten components become a content type of their own and are specified as they stand. The count was not driven down for its own sake: four further unifications were considered and the types kept distinct instead, and those four are set out in section 04 alongside the ones that were unified.</p>
  <p>Every content type has a matching component in the Next.js and Tailwind front end built alongside it, so the <Fact k="contentTypes" /> types resolve to <Fact k="authorableBlocks" /> block components, one section wrapper and two route-level page components. The container decision pays off twice over there: presentation props become a single class map on one component instead of being restated seventeen times, and <code>section.blocks</code> resolves through one registry, so a new type later is a content-model change plus one registry entry.</p>
  <p>Three types carry most of the delivery risk, and none of them is a modelling problem. The card collection, because query-driven collections are the least specified thing in any inventory. The lead capture form and the calculator, because their behaviour — progressive fields, submission, formula — is invisible from a rendered page and has to come from EG.</p>
</div>
</Section>

<Section n="04" id="decisions" title="Modelling decisions">
<div data-origin="uc2:decisions:*">
  <p class="sec-note">Ten decisions. Six bring components together under a single type; four keep types distinct where combining them would cost a validation or blur what an author reaches for. The four distinctions carry as much weight as the six unifications — together they show the count was reasoned rather than minimised.</p>
  <div class="scroll">
  <table>
    <thead><tr><th style="width:26%">Candidates</th><th style="width:13%">Outcome</th><th style="width:32%">Deciding test</th><th>Result</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>C008, C021, C032, C011 split banners</td><td><span class="pill p-merge">Unified</span></td><td>Same content model throughout; the differences are media position, an optional CTA and one optional block slot</td><td><code>CT08</code></td></tr>
      <tr><td>C013, C016, C031 collections</td><td><span class="pill p-merge">Unified</span></td><td>One container model; grid versus carousel is presentation, and the card type is a reference constraint</td><td><code>CT18</code></td></tr>
      <tr><td>C004, C022 heroes</td><td><span class="pill p-merge">Unified</span></td><td>The breadcrumb comes from the page; the remaining difference is one optional eyebrow field</td><td><code>CT07</code></td></tr>
      <tr><td>C012, C025 promo banners</td><td><span class="pill p-merge">Unified</span></td><td>Background treatment is presentation, as the analysis anticipated</td><td><code>CT09</code></td></tr>
      <tr><td>C027 body and footnotes</td><td><span class="pill p-merge">Unified</span></td><td>Same content model; the footnote is a rendering style</td><td><code>CT14</code> with <code>style</code></td></tr>
      <tr><td>C005, C019, C020</td><td><span class="pill p-abs">Structural</span></td><td>These carry no content of their own — they wrap, title and space the blocks inside them, and a heading stays tied to what it titles</td><td><code>CT01</code> — carries the presentation for every block</td></tr>
