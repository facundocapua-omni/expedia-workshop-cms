<h3>What drives the dates</h3>
<ul class="tight">
  <li><strong>A ramp takes about five weeks per business case</strong> — preparation and freeze, wave 0, 20%, 50%, 100% — because each step waits for a full weekly traffic cycle. Once the pipeline is proven, the ramps of different business cases can overlap; the limits are EG's QA capacity and how many content freezes can run at once.</li>
  <li><strong>The last business case</strong> reaches 100% no later than <em>contract end − buffer − rollback window</em> (Q34, Q35).</li>
  <li><strong>The brand refresh is on the critical path.</strong> Bulk migration waits for the refreshed model (D-27), so a slip there moves every date after it (Q27).</li>
</ul>

<div class="decide">
  <h4>An extension is negotiated only if the plan needs one</h4>
  <p>A checkpoint sits at <em>contract end − notice period − negotiation time</em>. At that date, the business cases still to move decide whether an AEM extension is negotiated. Without a dated checkpoint, "only if needed" tends to be discovered after the moment to act has passed.</p>
  <p>Business case waves are what make this workable. A shortfall is bounded and measurable — two business cases left, not "the migration" — and that is the difference between negotiating a short extension and renewing the contract.</p>
</div>
